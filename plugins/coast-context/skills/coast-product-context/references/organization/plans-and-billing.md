# Plans, usage, and billing

A **plan** is a commercial offering; an organization's **billing subscription** is its relationship to that offering over time. Subscription status, trial period, billing items, and management through checkout or a customer portal concern the commercial account. They are different from a person's [organization role or workspace permission](access.md).

## Commercial access and usage

An **entitlement** or plan restriction can affect whether an organization may use a capability or action. A **usage measure** counts consumption, such as records, messages, or storage. A **limit** describes an allowed amount or condition; an **overage** compares usage with that limit. Those concepts help explain a billing message or restricted experience without making the overage a permission change on an individual member.

Plan effects can appear in several parts of the product: record access, messaging, storage and uploads, and some actions. Enforcement is specific to the operation. A limit on one kind of usage does not prove that every operation is blocked the same way, or that existing data is deleted. Explain the effect observed for the customer's account and action rather than deriving it from a plan name alone.

## Deciding what applies

Three questions remain separate: whether the person is authorized for the object and action, whether the organization's commercial access permits the capability or usage, and whether that capability is available in the relevant client and deployment. A paid plan does not automatically grant a member workspace administration; a workspace grant does not override an organization's plan restriction.

This reference defines those distinctions, not current prices, quotas, promotions, or an individual customer's terms. Use confirmed account or team information for the current commercial detail. If it is unavailable, state what is unknown. Link existing commercial guidance when it is accessible to the reader.
